GCSE Chemistry — Equation Sheet
Edexcel (1CH0) • Equations, units and common errors from examiner reports
Atomic masses & formulae
Relative Atomic Mass (Ar)
H onlyP1 or P2
Ar = Σ(massn × abundancen) ÷ 100
If abundances are given as decimals, divide by 1 instead of 100
Ar = relative atomic mass (no units) • massn = mass number of isotope • abundancen = % abundance
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Rounding the answer to a whole number — e.g. writing 28 instead of 28.1 for silicon (1CH0/1H Nov 2021)
  • Not showing the multiplication step for each isotope — loses the working mark
  • Not dividing by 100 when abundances are given as percentages
  • Confusing this with empirical formula work — both use percentages but this is a weighted mean, not a moles ratio
Relative Formula Mass (Mr)
F+HP1 or P2
Mr = Σ(Ar × number of atoms of each element)
Multiply Ar by subscript, then by any bracket multiplier, before adding
No units • Include water of crystallisation if shown (e.g. •5H2O)
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Failing to multiply Ar by the subscript — e.g. Mr(CaCO3) = 68 instead of 100 (1CH0 Jun 2019)
  • Not multiplying every atom inside brackets by the bracket subscript — e.g. Mr(Mg(OH)2) = 41 instead of 58
  • Writing a unit (“g” or “g/mol”) on the final answer — Mr is unitless
  • Ignoring water of crystallisation in hydrated salts
Empirical & Molecular Formula
F+HP1 or P2
moles of each element = mass ÷ Ar
→ divide all mole values by the smallest → round to whole numbers
Molecular formula: n = Mr(compound) ÷ Mr(empirical unit) → multiply empirical formula by n
If ratio is 1.5 → ×2 • 1.33 → ×3 • 1.25 → ×4
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Multiplying mass × Ar instead of dividing mass ÷ Ar (1CH0/1F Jun 2019)
  • Inverting the fraction — dividing Ar ÷ mass (1CH0/1H Jun 2019)
  • Stopping at the ratio (e.g. “1:2:1”) without converting to a formula with subscripts — loses final mark (1CH0/2H Jun 2019)
  • Rounding 1.5 down to 1 rather than doubling all values to get integers
Percentage Composition (% by mass)
F+HP1 or P2
% by mass = (Ar × subscript) ÷ Mr × 100
Answer is a percentage — no mass unit on the final answer
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Using Ar alone instead of Ar × subscript — e.g. 16 for oxygen in CaCO3 instead of 48
  • Not rounding to the number of significant figures the question specifies — “a major reason candidates lost the final mark” (1CH0 Jun 2019)
  • Rounding mid-calculation instead of at the final step
Moles & amounts
The Mole
H onlyP1 or P2
moles = mass ÷ Mr
Rearrangements: mass = moles × Mr  |  Mr = mass ÷ moles
number of particles = moles × 6.02 × 1023
Rearrangement: moles = particles ÷ 6.02 × 1023
moles in mol • mass in g • 6.02 × 1023 mol−1 = Avogadro constant
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Quoting 6.02 × 1023 as the final answer without multiplying by atoms per formula unit (1CH0/1H Jun 2019 — CuI question)
  • Dividing the wrong way round — e.g. 159.5 ÷ 15.95 instead of 15.95 ÷ 159.5 (1CH0/1H Jun 2019)
  • Forgetting to convert milligrams to grams first (divide mg by 1000)
  • Entering 6.02 × 10 × 23 on the calculator — use the EXP button
Reacting Masses
F+HP1 or P2
Method A — Ratio
mass given ÷ Mr(given) = mass unknown ÷ Mr(unknown)
Method B — Moles (preferred at Higher)
moles = mass ÷ Mr → apply equation ratio → mass = moles × Mr
Always use coefficients from the balanced equation to set the ratio
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Calculating Mr of the product and writing that as the answer without scaling (1CH0/2F Jun 2019)
  • Not using the balancing numbers (coefficients) to set the ratio
  • Poor unstructured working — “the setting out of many candidates’ calculations was invariably poor” (1CH0/2F Jun 2019)
Limiting Reagents
H onlyP1 or P2
Step 1: moles of each reactant = mass ÷ Mr
Step 2: divide moles by its coefficient in the balanced equation
Step 3: smallest result = limiting reagent
Step 4: moles of product = moles(limiting) × (product coeff ÷ limiting coeff)
Step 5: mass of product = moles × Mr
Name the limiting reagent explicitly before calculating product mass
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Assuming all reactions are 1:1 — must check the balanced equation coefficients
  • Not naming which reagent is limiting before calculating
  • Using moles of the excess reagent instead of the limiting reagent
Concentrations
Concentration (g dm−3)
F+HP1 or P2
▶ Unit check first: volume in cm3? Divide by 1000 to get dm3.   1 dm3 = 1000 cm3 = 1 litre
c = m ÷ V
Rearrangements: m = c × V  |  V = m ÷ c
c = concentration (g dm−3) • m = mass of solute (g) • V = volume of solution (dm3)
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Not converting cm3 to dm3 — gives answer 1000× wrong; “the single most common error” (1CH0/2F Jun 2019)
  • Omitting the unit (g dm−3) from the final answer
  • Using the volume of solvent added rather than the total final volume of solution
Concentration (mol dm−3)
H onlySEPP1
▶ Unit check: cm3 ÷ 1000 = dm3  •  g dm−3 → mol dm−3: divide by Mr
C = n ÷ V
Rearrangements: n = C × V  |  V = n ÷ C
Converting: mol dm−3 = g dm−3 ÷ Mr
C = concentration (mol dm−3) • n = moles (mol) • V = volume (dm3)
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Forgetting to divide volume by 1000 — answer 1000× wrong (1CH0/1H Jun 2019)
  • Dividing Mr by concentration instead of concentration by Mr (1CH0/1H Jun 2019)
  • Not giving the answer to the decimal places specified (1CH0/1H Jun 2019)
Quantitative analysis — Separate Chemistry (1CH0) only
Percentage Yield
F+HSEPP1
% yield = (actual yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100
Both yields must be in the same units (usually g) before dividing
Answer is a % — never write g or mol • Expected range: 0–100% for a normal reaction
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Giving an “incomplete reaction / practical losses / side reactions” reason when asked why yield is greater than 100% — that question needs “product not fully dried” or “contains impurities” (1CH0/1H Nov 2020)
  • Not calculating theoretical yield from reacting masses first
  • Mixing units — actual yield in kg, theoretical in g (or vice versa)
Atom Economy
F+HSEPP1
% atom economy = (Mr of desired product ÷ total Mr of all products) × 100
Apply balancing coefficients: 2 mol of product → use 2 × Mr in numerator
Answer is a % — no units • Only reactions with one product can have 100% atom economy
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Writing 100% because “both products are useful” — uses desired product only (1CH0/1F Jun 2019)
  • Giving a bare answer without working — “a common answer was 56, which generally did not score” (1CH0/1F Jun 2019)
  • Confusing “1 decimal place” with “nearest whole number” — 80.5% ≠ 80% (1CH0/1H Nov 2020)
Titration Calculations
H onlySEPP1
▶ Before calculating: use concordant titres only (within 0.10 cm3) for the mean. Convert cm3 ÷ 1000 = dm3.
n = C × V    (V in dm3)
Rearrangements: C = n ÷ V  |  V = n ÷ C
Apply the mole ratio from the balanced equation to find moles of the unknown
Convert mol dm−3 → g dm−3: multiply by Mr
n = moles (mol) • C = concentration (mol dm−3) • V = volume (dm3)
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Forgetting to divide cm3 by 1000 — answer 1000× wrong (1CH0/1H Jun 2019)
  • Inverting the division when converting units — “159.5/15.95” flagged as wrong (1CH0/1H Jun 2019)
  • Assuming 1:1 mole ratio — check the equation (HCl:Ca(OH)2 = 2:1; H2SO4:NaOH = 1:2)
  • Including the rough titre in the mean — concordant titres only
  • Not rounding to the decimal places specified
Molar Volume of a Gas
H onlySEPP1 or P2
▶ Molar volume at rtp = 24 dm3 mol−1 (= 24,000 cm3 mol−1) at ≈20°C, 1 atm
V (dm3) = moles × 24
Rearrangement: moles = V (dm3) ÷ 24  |  then: mass = moles × Mr
V must be in dm3 — convert cm3 by dividing by 1000 first
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Dividing when converting moles to volume — “often divided by 24 when they should have multiplied” (1CH0/1H Jun 2019)
  • Not converting cm3 to dm3 before dividing by 24
  • Forgetting to scale O2 volume to air volume when asked (air ≈20% O2, so ×5) (1CH0/1H Jun 2019)
Avogadro’s Law — Gas Volume Ratios
H onlySEPP1 or P2
Volume ratio = Mole ratio (from balanced equation coefficients)
Only applies to gases (g) at the same temperature and pressure
Vunknown = Vknown × (coeffunknown ÷ coeffknown)
Units stay the same throughout (both dm3 or both cm3) • Exclude solids and liquids
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Dividing when the larger-coefficient gas requires multiplying (1CH0/1H Jun 2019)
  • Applying the ratio to non-gaseous species — only state symbol (g) counts
  • Adding CO2 volume but ignoring H2O vapour when “total gaseous products” is asked (1CH0/1H Jun 2024)
Rates & energy
Rate of Reaction
F+HP2
Rate = change in quantity ÷ time
Gas collected: Rate (cm3/s) = volume of gas (cm3) ÷ time (s)
Mass loss: Rate (g/s) = mass lost (g) ÷ time (s)
Mean rate uses total change over total time • Initial rate = gradient at time = 0 (steepest part)
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Missing the bung from apparatus diagrams — “candidates lost marks by missing the bung” (1CH0/2H Jun 2019)
  • Not labelling apparatus scientifically (1CH0/2H Jun 2019)
  • Calculating mean rate over the whole experiment when initial rate is asked for
Bond Energy Calculation
H onlyP2
ΔH = Σ(bond energies broken) − Σ(bond energies made)
ΔH = (energy in) − (energy out)
Negative ΔH = exothermic  •  Positive ΔH = endothermic
ΔH in kJ mol−1 • Multiply each bond energy by the number of those bonds • Data table provided
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Forgetting the minus sign — “most common cause of lost marks: 730 instead of −730” (1CH0/2H Nov 2021)
  • Doing the subtraction the wrong way round (made − broken gives right magnitude, wrong sign)
  • Not multiplying bond energy by the number of bonds — CH4 has 4 C–H bonds; 2H2O has 4 O–H bonds
  • Misidentifying double bonds as single (C=C ≠ C–C; O=O ≠ O–O)
  • Transcription errors between calculator and paper (1CH0/2H Nov 2021)
Heat of Combustion (Core Practical)
F+HSEPP2
mass burnt = mass of burner (before) − mass of burner (after)
temperature rise (ΔT) = Tfinal − Tinitial
mass in g • temperature in °C • Weigh burner + lid each time
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Confusing “stop at 30°C” with “a temperature rise of 30°C” — it is the rise that must equal the target (1CH0/2F Jun 2019)
  • Not replacing the lid immediately after lighting — evaporation loss makes the mass change too large
  • Confusing mass of the whole burner with mass of alcohol only (1CH0/2F Jun 2019)
Rf Value (Chromatography)
F+HP1 or P2
Rf = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent front
Both distances measured from the baseline • Measure to the centre of the spot
No units • Always between 0 and 1 • Quote to 2 significant figures
⚠︎ Common errors
  • Not recalling the formula — “some candidates were unable to recall the correct formula for Rf” (1CH0/1F Jun 2019)
  • Not giving the answer to 2 significant figures — “failing to give 0.29 to required 2 s.f.” (1CH0/1F Jun 2019)
  • Measuring from the bottom of the paper rather than the pencil baseline
  • Measuring to the top or edge of the spot rather than its centre
