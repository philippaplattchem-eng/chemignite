CP6 Acid-Alkali Titration

Foundation Tier

Tier: Examined on Foundation and Higher tier

Qualification: Separate Chemistry (1CH0) only, not assessed in Combined Science (1SC0)

Paper: Paper 1

Specification: 5.9C. Core Practical: Carry out an accurate acid-alkali titration, using burette, pipette and a suitable indicator.

Total marks: 20

This Core Practical is the accurate titration of an acid against an alkali to find the exact volume of acid needed to neutralise a known volume of alkali. The June 2019 1CH0/1F examiner report flagged that lower-band candidates confuse the names of key apparatus (burette vs pipette) and forget to describe both the rough and the accurate titration stages.

Key Revision Notes

Part 1. Apparatus

  1. The burette holds the acid (usually HCl). It is a tall narrow glass tube with a tap at the bottom, and a graduated scale that lets you read the volume to the nearest 0.05 cm3.
  2. The pipette measures an exact, fixed volume of alkali (usually 25.0 cm3) into the conical flask. A pipette filler is used to draw the liquid up safely, never use your mouth.
  3. The conical flask holds the alkali. Its shape lets you swirl the contents without splashing.
  4. A white tile is placed under the flask so that the indicator colour change is easy to see.
  5. The funnel is used to fill the burette, then removed before titrating so that drips don't add extra acid uncounted.
  6. A retort stand with a burette clamp holds the burette vertical and steady.

Part 2. Method

  1. Use the pipette and pipette filler to transfer exactly 25.0 cm3 of alkali into the conical flask.
  2. Add 2–3 drops of indicator (e.g. phenolphthalein). The alkali turns pink.
  3. Fill the burette with acid using a funnel. Remove the funnel. Record the initial burette reading from the bottom of the meniscus, at eye level.
  4. Rough titration: open the tap and run acid into the flask, swirling continuously, until the indicator just turns colourless. Record the rough titre.
  5. Accurate titrations: repeat with a fresh 25.0 cm3 of alkali. Run acid quickly to within ~1–2 cm3 of the rough titre, then add dropwise until the colour just disappears.
  6. Repeat until you have at least two concordant titres (within 0.10 cm3 of each other). Discard the rough titre and any anomalies, then calculate the mean of the concordant accurate titres.

Part 3. Indicator Choice

  1. Phenolphthalein: pink in alkali, colourless in acid, the standard choice for HCl + NaOH.
  2. Methyl orange: yellow in alkali, red in acid, used for strong acid + weak alkali.
  3. Universal indicator is not used in titrations because it has a gradual range of colours rather than a sharp end-point.

Mark Scheme and Guidance

Section A. Multiple Choice

Question 1. Apparatus for measuring 25.0 cm3 of alkali.
Mark Scheme and Guidance

D. Pipette [1 mark]

A is incorrect. A beaker has very rough graduations, not accurate enough for titrations.

B is incorrect. The burette holds the acid (the solution being added), not the alkali.

C is incorrect. A measuring cylinder is less precise than a pipette and is not normally used in titrations.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The pipette is a fixed-volume piece of glassware, a 25 cm3 pipette delivers exactly 25.0 cm3, no more and no less. It is the most accurate way to measure a fixed volume of liquid at GCSE.

Always use a pipette filler to draw the liquid up. Never use your mouth.

Question 2. Why remove the funnel?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

B. To stop drips from the funnel adding extra acid that is not counted in the titre [1 mark]

A is incorrect. The funnel does not stabilise the burette, the burette clamp does.

C is incorrect. The burette is meant to hold the acid and release it through the tap, not let it escape from the top.

D is incorrect. Allowing air in is not a goal, the burette is open to air at the top anyway.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

Once the burette is full, take the funnel off and place it on the bench. If you leave it in the burette during the titration, even a few drops will fall into the burette uncounted and your titre will be artificially small.

Question 3. Suitable indicator for HCl + NaOH.
Mark Scheme and Guidance

B. Phenolphthalein [1 mark]

A is incorrect. Litmus paper changes colour over a wide pH range, the change is too gradual to give a sharp end-point.

C is incorrect. Universal indicator gives a continuous range of colours (red → orange → yellow → green → blue → purple), so there is no single sharp colour change at the end-point.

D is incorrect. Bromine water is used to test for alkenes, not as an acid-alkali indicator.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The two suitable indicators at GCSE are phenolphthalein (pink in alkali, colourless in acid) and methyl orange (yellow in alkali, red in acid). Both give a sharp colour change at the end-point.

Question 4. Why do a rough titration first?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

B. To find the approximate end-point so that the accurate runs can be added dropwise near that volume [1 mark]

A is incorrect. The rough titration is not about wasting acid, it is about finding the end-point quickly.

C is incorrect. The indicator behaves the same way every time. The rough titration does not change the indicator.

D is incorrect. Cleaning the burette is done with water and then a rinse with the acid, not with a titration.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The rough titration tells you roughly how much acid is needed (e.g. about 24 cm3). In the accurate runs, you can then add ~22 cm3 quickly and then slow right down to dropwise additions for the last 2 cm3. This saves time and stops you overshooting the end-point.

Question 5. Which titre should be left out?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

D. 24.65 cm3 [1 mark]

A, B and C are incorrect. The other three titres (24.10, 24.15 and 24.20 cm3) are all within 0.10 cm3 of each other, so they are concordant and should all be used.

The titre 24.65 cm3 is more than 0.45 cm3 away from the others, it is anomalous and should be discarded.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

"Concordant" means the values agree to within 0.10 cm3 of each other. When working out the mean titre, include only the concordant values, never the rough run, and never any clear anomalies.

Section B. Structured Question

Question (a)(i). The acid is added from the … [1 mark]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The correct piece of apparatus is:

  • Burette [1 mark]

"Pipette" is not accepted. The pipette delivers the alkali into the flask.

"Measuring cylinder" is not accepted. It is not accurate enough for titrations.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The 1CH0/1F June 2019 examiner report flagged that candidates frequently confuse the names of the apparatus, especially burette and pipette. Memorise it this way:

  • Burette (long, narrow, tap at the bottom) delivers the acid
  • Pipette (long, thin, no tap) measures the alkali
Question (a)(ii). 25.0 cm3 of alkali is measured using a … [1 mark]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The correct piece of apparatus is:

  • Pipette [1 mark]

"Burette" is not accepted. The burette holds the acid.

"Measuring cylinder" is not accepted. A pipette is more accurate.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The pipette gives a fixed, precise volume (25.0 cm3) every time, with very small uncertainty. A measuring cylinder is much less precise and would introduce a much larger error.

Question (b). Colour change of phenolphthalein. [2 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The colour change is:

  • From pink … [1 mark]
  • … to colourless [1 mark]

"Clear" is accepted in place of "colourless".

"Red" is not accepted. Phenolphthalein in alkali is pink, not red.

"White" is not accepted. The solution becomes colourless (transparent), not white.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The order of the colour change matters. The alkali is in the flask first, so it goes pink (alkaline). Acid is then added, at the end-point, the solution is just neutralised, so the pink colour disappears.

One drop of acid is enough to discharge the pink colour completely at the end-point, which is why you slow down to dropwise additions near the end of each accurate titration.

Question (c). Why universal indicator is not used. [1 mark]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

Any one from:

  • The colour change is gradual / not sharp [1 mark]
  • It changes through many colours rather than giving a single sharp end-point
  • You cannot tell exactly when the end-point has been reached

"It is not accurate" is not accepted on its own, the reason must reference the gradual colour change or the absence of a sharp end-point.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

Universal indicator is brilliant for showing approximate pH (e.g. "this solution is around pH 4") but useless for titrations. In a titration you need to detect a single moment (the end-point) and universal indicator's smooth colour change makes that moment impossible to pin down.

Question (d)(i). Which two titres are concordant? [1 mark]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The concordant titres are:

  • Titration 1 (24.25 cm3) and Titration 3 (24.25 cm3) [1 mark]

"Rough and Titration 1" is not accepted. The rough titre is always discarded.

"Titration 1 and Titration 2" is not accepted. 24.25 and 24.20 differ by 0.05 cm3, which is within concordant range, but Titration 3 also matches Titration 1 exactly, so the marker is looking for the best matching pair.

Accept also "Titration 1, 2 and 3" (24.25, 24.20, 24.25), all three are within 0.10 cm3.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

"Concordant" means within 0.10 cm3. Scan the volume-of-acid row, ignore the rough run, and look for the run(s) that match each other most closely.

Question (d)(ii). Calculate the mean titre. [2 marks]

Model Answer

To calculate the mean titre using the two concordant results (Titration 1 and Titration 3):

  • Add the two concordant titres:

    24.25 + 24.25 = 48.50

  • Divide by 2:

    mean titre = 48.50 ÷ 2

    mean titre = 24.25 [1 mark]

  • State the answer to 2 decimal places, with the correct unit:

    mean titre = 24.25 cm3 [1 mark]

Mark Scheme and Guidance

1 mark for the correct calculation (48.50 ÷ 2 = 24.25).

1 mark for the final answer with correct unit cm3, to 2 decimal places.

If three concordant titres are used (24.25 + 24.20 + 24.25) ÷ 3 = 24.233..., accept 24.23 cm3.

"24.25" with no unit is not accepted for the second mark.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

Always quote titres and mean titres to two decimal places (e.g. 24.25, not 24.3), the burette can be read to 0.05 cm3, so the second decimal place must always be either 0 or 5.

Always include the unit (cm3), missing units is one of the most common avoidable mark losses on calculation questions.

Question (e). Place the steps in the correct order. [2 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The correct order is:

C → A → E → B → D

  • 2 marks for the fully correct order (CAEBD)
  • 1 mark if 4 of the 5 letters are in the correct position (i.e. one letter out of place)

The reasoning:

  1. C, Pipette alkali into the flask first
  2. A, Add indicator to the alkali in the flask
  3. E, Fill the burette and remove the funnel
  4. B, Record the initial burette reading
  5. D, Run acid in, swirling, until the colour disappears
Examiner Tips and Tricks

This is a classic Foundation sequencing question (modelled on 1CH0/1H November 2020 Q10(a)(i)). The key logical jump is that you set up the flask first (alkali + indicator), then prepare the burette (fill + reading), then carry out the titration. You can't read the burette reading until the burette is filled, and you can't observe a colour change until indicator has been added.

Question (f). Why do a rough titration first? [2 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

Any two of the following (1 mark each):

  • The rough titration gives the approximate volume of acid needed to reach the end-point
  • So that, in the accurate titrations, acid can be added quickly at first / quickly up to that volume
  • Then added dropwise near the end-point
  • To avoid overshooting the end-point
  • To save time on each accurate run

[2 marks]

"To get a rough idea" is not enough on its own, the answer must explain how this helps the accurate runs.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The two-mark version of this answer always links the rough titration to a benefit for the accurate run. A good template:

"The rough titration finds the approximate end-point volume (1 mark) so that the accurate runs can be added dropwise near that volume to avoid overshooting (1 mark)."

Question (g). Two safety precautions. [2 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

Any two from (1 mark each):

  • Wear safety goggles / eye protection (acid and alkali are irritants)
  • Use a pipette filler, never pipette by mouth
  • Wipe up any spills of acid or alkali with a damp cloth
  • Wash hands after handling the chemicals
  • Clamp the burette so it cannot fall over
  • Stand up to take the burette reading (so you don't drop the burette while leaning over)

[2 marks]

"Be careful" alone is not accepted.

"Wear a lab coat" is generally not credited at GCSE without a specific reason.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

Two safe answers for almost any acid/alkali practical: wear safety goggles (eye protection from splashes) and use a pipette filler (never use your mouth on a pipette).

Question (h). Why not use a measuring cylinder? [1 mark]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

Any one from:

  • A measuring cylinder is not accurate / precise enough [1 mark]
  • A pipette measures the volume to a smaller uncertainty / more decimal places
  • A measuring cylinder has wider graduations than a pipette

"It's the wrong shape" is not accepted. The reason must reference accuracy or precision.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

A 25 cm3 pipette is calibrated to deliver exactly 25.00 cm3 with very small uncertainty (typically ±0.06 cm3). A 25 cm3 measuring cylinder has divisions of about 0.5 cm3, so the uncertainty is ten times larger. For accurate titrations, you need the pipette.
