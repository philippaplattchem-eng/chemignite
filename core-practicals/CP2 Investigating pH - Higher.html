CP2 Investigating pH

Higher Tier

Tier: Examined on Foundation and Higher tier

Qualification: Combined Science (1SC0) and Separate Chemistry (1CH0)

Paper: Paper 1

Specification: 3.6. Investigate the change in pH on adding powdered calcium hydroxide or calcium oxide to a fixed volume of dilute hydrochloric acid.

Total marks: 20

This Core Practical investigates how the pH of a dilute acid changes as a powdered base is added. The chemistry is simple neutralisation, but the practical skills tested go further: identifying variables, plotting an S-shaped curve, and explaining the pH change at a particle level. The November 2020 examiner report singled out one mark as the discriminator: candidates must explicitly state that the concentration of H⁺ ions decreases.

Key Revision Notes

The expected shape of the pH curve: flat-low region, steep climb through the equivalence point, then flat-high region around pH 12 (because calcium hydroxide is only sparingly soluble).
  1. The practical investigates the pH change when powdered calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)2, or calcium oxide, CaO, is added a spatula at a time to a fixed volume of dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl).
  2. The reaction is neutralisation: Ca(OH)2 (s) + 2HCl (aq) → CaCl2 (aq) + 2H2O (l). The same products form from CaO.
  3. Variables. Independent: mass (or spatula measures) of base added. Dependent: pH of the mixture. Controls: volume and concentration of HCl, identity and particle size of the base, stirring time, temperature.
  4. Measure pH using a pH probe (one decimal place) or universal indicator paper (whole-number pH only). A pH probe is more accurate because it gives one-decimal-place readings instead of just whole numbers from a colour chart.
  5. The pH increases as more base is added. The plotted curve is an S-shape: flat-low at first, steep through the equivalence point (around pH 7), then flat-high above pH 7.
  6. The curve plateaus near pH 12 because calcium hydroxide is only sparingly soluble. Once the solution is saturated with Ca(OH)2, any extra base sits as undissolved solid and the pH cannot rise any further.
  7. Particle-level explanation (Higher discriminator). The base supplies OH− ions. These react with H+ ions in the acid: H+ (aq) + OH− (aq) → H2O (l). Each addition removes more H+ ions, so the concentration of H+ ions falls. Because pH measures the concentration of H+, the pH rises.
  8. The November 2020 Higher examiner report flagged the top mark for stating that the concentration of H+ ions decreases. Saying only "the pH increases" or "magnesium hydroxide neutralises the acid" did not gain the discriminator mark.
  9. Variants. Magnesium hydroxide, Mg(OH)2, and barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)2, are valid exam substitutes for the named bases. The method, the variables, and the shape of the curve are unchanged.
  10. Safety: wear goggles throughout. Calcium oxide reacts exothermically with water, so add it slowly to avoid splashing. Universal indicator solution is flammable when alcohol-based.
  11. To plot the graph: independent variable on the x-axis, dependent on the y-axis. Both axes labelled with quantity and unit (pH has no unit). Use a scale that fills more than half the grid. Plot points within half a small square. Draw a smooth curve of best fit, not dot-to-dot.
  12. Common candidate errors flagged by examiner reports: saying the pH falls (it rises), copying "in excess" straight from the stem (no credit), and not stating the H+ concentration change.

Section A. Multiple Choice (5 marks)

1. A student adds spatula measures of powdered calcium hydroxide to a fixed volume of dilute hydrochloric acid and records the pH after each addition. Which is the dependent variable in this investigation? [1 mark]

  1. The volume of dilute hydrochloric acid
  2. The mass of calcium hydroxide added
  3. The pH of the mixture
  4. The temperature of the laboratory

2. Which is the balanced equation for the reaction between calcium hydroxide and dilute hydrochloric acid? [1 mark]

  1. Ca(OH)2 + HCl → CaCl + H2O
  2. Ca(OH)2 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + 2H2O
  3. Ca(OH)2 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2
  4. Ca + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2

3. A student plots a graph of pH against mass of calcium hydroxide added. What is the expected shape of the curve? [1 mark]

  1. A straight line with a negative gradient
  2. A bell-shaped curve that peaks and then falls
  3. An S-shape: flat at low pH, steep rise through the equivalence point, then flat at high pH
  4. A straight line with a positive gradient that rises continuously

4. After many additions of calcium hydroxide, the pH stops changing at around pH 12. Which is the best explanation for this plateau? [1 mark]

  1. All the H+ ions have been neutralised, and any extra Ca(OH)2 is only sparingly soluble, so the pH cannot rise any further
  2. The thermometer reading has reached its maximum value
  3. All the calcium hydroxide has reacted and no more is left to add
  4. The water in the solution has evaporated

5. Which is the most accurate piece of equipment for measuring the pH of the mixture after each addition? [1 mark]

  1. Litmus paper, because it gives a clear red or blue reading
  2. Universal indicator paper, because the colour chart is easy to read
  3. A calibrated pH probe, because it gives a one-decimal-place reading
  4. A thermometer, because pH depends on temperature

Section B. Structured Question (15 marks)

A student investigates how the pH of dilute hydrochloric acid changes as powdered calcium hydroxide is added. She measures 25 cm³ of 1.0 mol/dm³ HCl into a conical flask, then adds Ca(OH)2 one spatula measure at a time, stirring after each addition and recording the pH using a calibrated pH probe.

(a) Identify the independent variable in this investigation. [1 mark]

(b) Give two control variables the student should keep the same in this investigation. [2 marks]

(c) Sketch the expected shape of the curve when pH (y-axis) is plotted against the mass of calcium hydroxide added (x-axis). Label the starting pH and the final pH on your sketch. [3 marks]

(d) Explain, in terms of ions, why the pH of the mixture increases as more calcium hydroxide is added. [4 marks]

(e) After many additions, the pH levels off at around pH 12 and does not increase further, even when more calcium hydroxide is added. Explain why. [2 marks]

(f) The student repeats the experiment using barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)2, instead of calcium hydroxide. Barium hydroxide is more soluble than calcium hydroxide. Suggest, with a reason, how the shape of the new graph would differ from the original. [2 marks]

(g) Suggest one improvement the student could make to the experiment to obtain more reliable results, other than using a different base. [1 mark]

Mark Scheme and Guidance

Section A. Multiple Choice

Question 1. Which is the dependent variable in this investigation?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

C. The pH of the mixture [1 mark]

A is incorrect. The volume of HCl is kept the same throughout, so it is a control variable.

B is incorrect. The mass of calcium hydroxide added is what the student deliberately changes, so it is the independent variable.

D is incorrect. The temperature of the laboratory is kept constant, so it is a control variable.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The quick test for the dependent variable is: what is being measured after each change? In this practical the student records pH after every addition, so pH is the dependent variable.

Independent is what you change. Dependent is what you measure. Controls are what you keep the same.

Question 2. Which is the balanced equation?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

B. Ca(OH)2 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + 2H2O [1 mark]

A is incorrect. The formula CaCl is wrong. Calcium forms a 2+ ion and chloride a 1− ion, so the formula must be CaCl2.

C is incorrect. The product of acid + base is salt + water, not salt + hydrogen. Hydrogen gas is the product of acid + reactive metal.

D is incorrect. The reactant is calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)2, not calcium metal, Ca. Calcium metal would react with water immediately.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

Always check the formula of the salt produced. Calcium has a charge of 2+ and chloride 1−, so calcium chloride is CaCl2, never CaCl.

Acid + base gives salt + water. Acid + metal gives salt + hydrogen. Knowing which product set goes with which reactant pair stops you picking distractors C and D.

Question 3. What is the expected shape of the curve?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

C. An S-shape: flat at low pH, steep rise through the equivalence point, then flat at high pH [1 mark]

A is incorrect. The pH rises as base is added, so the gradient is positive, not negative.

B is incorrect. The pH does not fall again after the rise. Once the acid is neutralised, more base only raises the pH (to a limit).

D is incorrect. The curve is not a straight line. It is flat at the start, steep through the equivalence point, then flat again at the plateau.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

Picture the experiment. At the start there is a huge excess of H+, so adding a small amount of base barely changes the pH (flat-low region). As you approach the equivalence point, each addition removes a much larger proportion of the remaining H+, so the pH climbs sharply. Once the acid is gone, the pH plateaus because calcium hydroxide is only sparingly soluble.

That gives you the classic S-shape. Never draw a straight line for this practical.

Question 4. Why does the pH plateau at pH 12?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

A. All the H+ ions have been neutralised, and any extra Ca(OH)2 is only sparingly soluble, so the pH cannot rise any further [1 mark]

B is incorrect. The thermometer reading is not relevant. pH is being measured, not temperature.

C is incorrect. The student is still adding more calcium hydroxide. It is the acid that has been used up, not the base.

D is incorrect. The water has not evaporated. The experiment is done at room temperature and the flask is not heated.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The pH 12 plateau comes from a real chemistry constraint: Ca(OH)2 is only sparingly soluble in water. Once the solution is saturated with Ca(OH)2, any extra base sits at the bottom of the flask as undissolved solid and does not increase the concentration of OH− ions in solution.

If the base were more soluble (for example, barium hydroxide), the plateau pH would be higher.

Question 5. Which is the most accurate piece of equipment for measuring pH?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

C. A calibrated pH probe, because it gives a one-decimal-place reading [1 mark]

A is incorrect. Litmus paper only tells you if a solution is acidic or alkaline. It does not give a numerical pH value.

B is incorrect. Universal indicator paper gives only whole-number pH values, which are less precise than a pH probe.

D is incorrect. A thermometer measures temperature, not pH.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

When asked about accuracy, think about resolution: how many decimal places can the instrument resolve? A pH probe reads to 0.1 of a unit. Universal indicator paper only resolves to whole numbers (or sometimes half-numbers if the colour chart is detailed).

For a graph-plotting practical like this, one-decimal-place resolution makes the points easier to plot accurately and the curve smoother.

Section B. Structured Question

Question (a). Identify the independent variable. [1 mark]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The independent variable is:

  • Mass of calcium hydroxide added / number of spatula measures of calcium hydroxide added [1 mark]

"Calcium hydroxide" on its own is not accepted. The variable is the quantity being changed, not the substance.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

An independent variable is the one the student deliberately changes. Here the student adds more and more Ca(OH)2 after each pH reading, so the quantity of Ca(OH)2 (mass or spatula measures) is what is being changed.

Question (b). Give two control variables. [2 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

Any two from (1 mark for each):

  • Volume of dilute hydrochloric acid
  • Concentration of dilute hydrochloric acid
  • Particle size of the calcium hydroxide
  • Temperature of the mixture
  • Stirring time after each addition
  • The base used (Ca(OH)2 throughout, not switching mid-experiment)

[2 marks]

"pH" is not accepted as a control variable. pH is the dependent variable.

"Mass of calcium hydroxide" is not accepted as a control. That is the independent variable.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

Particle size is a particularly important control here. A finer powder has more surface area and reacts faster, so using the same source of Ca(OH)2 throughout means each addition reacts at a similar rate.

Pick two safe controls and write each as one short phrase. Marks are not awarded for vague phrases like "keep the apparatus the same".

Question (c). Sketch the expected shape of the curve. Label the starting pH and the final pH. [3 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

For the sketch:

  • Starting pH labelled near pH 1 (accept any value 1–2) [1 mark]
  • Curve has an S-shape: flat region at low pH, steep rise through pH 7, then flat region at high pH [1 mark]
  • Final plateau labelled near pH 12 (accept any value 11–13) [1 mark]

A straight diagonal line is not accepted. The curve must show three regions: flat, steep, flat.

Plotting individual points is not accepted as a sketch. The mark is for the overall shape.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The three regions of the S-curve match the three stages of the experiment. Flat-low: excess acid, pH changes very little. Steep: near the equivalence point, each addition removes a large fraction of the remaining H+. Flat-high: excess base, pH limited by the low solubility of Ca(OH)2.

Always label your axes. y-axis: pH (no unit). x-axis: mass of Ca(OH)2 / g (or number of spatula measures).

Question (d). Explain in terms of ions why the pH increases as more calcium hydroxide is added. [4 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The pH increases because:

  • The calcium hydroxide supplies OH− ions to the mixture [1 mark]
  • The OH− ions react with H+ ions in the acid to form water: H+ (aq) + OH− (aq) → H2O (l) [1 mark]
  • This decreases the concentration of H+ ions in the solution [1 mark]
  • Because pH is a measure of the concentration of H+ ions, the pH rises (above 7 once all the acid has reacted) [1 mark]

"The pH increases because the base neutralises the acid" alone does not gain credit. The answer must refer to ions.

Copying "in excess" or other phrases from the stem does not gain credit. The marker is looking for chemistry, not paraphrase.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

This is the exact question type the November 2020 Higher examiner report flagged. The discriminator mark was for stating that the concentration of H+ ions decreases. Students who said only "pH increases" or "the base neutralises the acid" lost it.

Structure your answer as a four-step chain: base supplies OH−, OH− reacts with H+, this lowers H+ concentration, and lower H+ concentration means higher pH. Tick each step off as you write.

Question (e). Explain why the pH levels off at around pH 12. [2 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The pH plateaus near pH 12 because:

  • All of the acid has been neutralised, so adding more base has no acid left to react with [1 mark]
  • Calcium hydroxide is only sparingly soluble in water, so any extra Ca(OH)2 sits as undissolved solid and does not raise the OH− concentration in solution any further [1 mark]

"Because Ca(OH)2 is in excess" alone is not enough. The reason for the plateau is the limited solubility of Ca(OH)2.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The reason for the plateau is a chemistry constraint, not a stopping point. Calcium hydroxide has a low solubility in water (about 0.02 mol/dm³ at room temperature). Once the solution is saturated with Ca(OH)2, no more dissolves and the OH− concentration in solution cannot rise.

You can verify this by looking at the pH of a saturated Ca(OH)2 solution (limewater): it is around pH 12, which matches the plateau on the graph.

Question (f). Compare the graph using barium hydroxide with the original. [2 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The shape of the graph using barium hydroxide compared with calcium hydroxide:

  • The plateau pH is higher (the curve levels off at a higher pH than 12) [1 mark]
  • Because barium hydroxide is more soluble than calcium hydroxide, so a higher concentration of OH− ions can be reached in solution [1 mark]

"The graph would be the same" is not accepted. The greater solubility of Ba(OH)2 changes the plateau height.

Saying only "the pH is higher" without explaining why does not gain the second mark.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

When a question asks you to compare with a variant (like Ba(OH)2 instead of Ca(OH)2), look at what is different about the variant. Here, barium hydroxide is described as more soluble. More soluble means more OH− can stay in solution, so the plateau pH is higher.

Always pair the change in the graph with the underlying chemistry reason. One mark for the observation, one mark for the explanation.

Question (g). Suggest one improvement to obtain more reliable results. [1 mark]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

Any one from:

  • Weigh out a fixed mass of base each time (rather than using spatula measures), so the x-axis values are reproducible
  • Repeat the experiment and average the results / take more readings to identify and remove anomalies
  • Use a calibrated pH probe (if not already used) for one-decimal-place readings
  • Stir for a fixed time after each addition so the reading is taken at consistent reaction completeness
  • Use the same source of finely powdered base throughout so particle size is constant

[1 mark]

"Use better equipment" alone is not accepted. The improvement must be specific.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

"Reliable" in chemistry exams usually means reproducible. The cleanest improvement here is to weigh out fixed masses instead of using spatula measures. A spatula measure is not a precise quantity, so the x-axis values vary between additions even if the student is careful.

Repeating the experiment and averaging is another safe answer that always works for "reliability" questions.
