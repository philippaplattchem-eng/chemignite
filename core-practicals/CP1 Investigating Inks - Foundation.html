CP1 Investigating Inks

Foundation Tier

Tier: Examined on Foundation and Higher tier

Qualification: Combined Science (1SC0) and Separate Chemistry (1CH0)

Paper: Paper 1

Specification: 2.11. Investigate the composition of inks using simple distillation and paper chromatography.

Total marks: 20

This Core Practical is about finding out what is in an ink. Ink is a mixture, made up of a liquid solvent (often water or ethanol) with one or more coloured dyes dissolved in it. Two methods are used. Simple distillation separates the solvent from the dyes. Paper chromatography separates the dyes from each other.

Key Revision Notes

Part A. Simple Distillation (recovering the solvent)

Past-paper distillation apparatus used to separate water from ink (Edexcel 1CH0/1F Paper 1, June 2018, Figure 9). Point A is inside the condenser, where the water is a gas (vapour). Point B is at the collecting end, where the water has cooled and turned back into a liquid.
  1. The practical answers two questions about an ink. What is the solvent? Simple distillation tells you. What dyes are in the ink? Paper chromatography tells you.
  2. The thermometer bulb sits at the side-arm. The temperature shown is the boiling point of the solvent. Around 100 °C means water. Around 78 °C means ethanol.
  3. Cold water flows through the condenser. The water goes in at the bottom and comes out at the top.
  4. The receiving end is always open to the atmosphere. If it was sealed, pressure could build up and crack the glass.
  5. Anti-bumping granules are added before heating. They stop the boiling liquid from spitting out of the flask.
  6. The dyes are left behind in the flask. They have very high boiling points, so they do not evaporate.

Part B. Paper Chromatography (separating the dyes)

Past-paper chromatogram from Edexcel 1CH0/1F Paper 1, June 2019, Figure 6. Five food colourings (P, Q, R, S, T) were placed on the pencil baseline. As the solvent rose, the dyes separated. The line near the top of the paper is the solvent front.
  1. The baseline is drawn in pencil. Pen ink would dissolve and run up the paper, ruining the chromatogram.
  2. The solvent level in the beaker must be below the baseline. If the solvent covered the spots, the dyes would wash off into the beaker.
  3. A lid is placed on the beaker so the solvent does not evaporate during the run.
  4. Rf = distance moved by dye ÷ distance moved by solvent front. Both distances are measured from the baseline. Give Rf to 2 significant figures.
  5. Rf values are always between 0 and 1. A spot that has not moved off the baseline has an Rf of 0, which means the dye did not dissolve in the solvent.
  6. You can identify a dye by matching its Rf (or spot position) to a known reference dye run on the same paper in the same solvent.

  4. The melting point of the ink

  2. distance moved by dye ÷ distance moved by solvent
  3. distance moved by dye + distance moved by solvent
  4. distance moved by dye × distance moved by solvent

  1. Pencil is easier to draw with
  2. Pencil does not dissolve in the solvent and stays in place
  3. Ink is hard to see on the paper
  4. Pencil reacts with the dyes

4. A student places a chromatography paper into a beaker of solvent. The solvent level is above the baseline. What will happen? [1 mark]

  1. The dye spots will dissolve into the solvent in the beaker
  2. The solvent will rise faster
  3. The Rf values will be more accurate
  4. The paper will not become wet

5. One dye in the ink stays on the baseline at the end of the experiment. What does this tell you about the dye? [1 mark]

  1. The dye is the most soluble in the solvent
  2. The dye did not dissolve in the solvent
  3. The dye has the highest Rf value
  4. The dye reached the solvent front

Section B. Structured Question (15 marks)

A student investigates a black ink. She thinks the ink is a mixture of dyes dissolved in water. She uses simple distillation to recover the solvent. She then uses paper chromatography to look at the dyes.

Mark Scheme and Guidance

Section A. Multiple Choice

Question 1. What does the thermometer reading tell the student?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

C. The boiling point of the solvent leaving the flask [1 mark]

A is incorrect. The thermometer is not in the flame and does not measure the flame temperature.

B is incorrect. The dye stays in the flask and does not vaporise, so the thermometer does not read the dye's boiling point.

D is incorrect. The ink does not melt in this practical. The solvent boils and the dyes remain in the flask.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The thermometer is at the side-arm exit for a reason. It reads the temperature of the vapour as it leaves the flask. That temperature is the boiling point of the solvent, and the boiling point tells you what the solvent is.

For water, the reading is around 100 °C. For ethanol, the reading is around 78 °C. Learning these two values is a fast mark in any distillation question.

Question 2. Which is the correct formula for the Rf value of a dye?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

B. distance moved by dye ÷ distance moved by solvent [1 mark]

A is incorrect. This is the formula upside down. It would give a value greater than 1, which is not possible for Rf.

C is incorrect. Rf is a ratio, so the formula uses division, not addition.

D is incorrect. Rf is a ratio, so the formula uses division, not multiplication.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

Rf is always a number between 0 and 1, because a dye cannot move further than the solvent that is carrying it.

An easy way to remember the formula is "dye over solvent, both from the baseline."

Question 3. Why is the baseline drawn in pencil and not in ink?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

B. Pencil does not dissolve in the solvent and stays in place [1 mark]

A is incorrect. Ease of drawing is not the reason. The reason is to do with the chemistry of pencil versus ink.

C is incorrect. Ink is easy to see, so visibility is not the problem. The problem is that ink dissolves in the solvent.

D is incorrect. Pencil does not react with the dyes. It stays in place because it is insoluble in the solvent.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

Pen ink is a mixture of dyes dissolved in a solvent (just like the ink being investigated in this practical). If you drew the baseline in pen, the solvent would dissolve that pen ink and carry it up the paper too, mixing in with the dyes you are trying to study.

Pencil is made of graphite, which is insoluble in water and most other chromatography solvents. The pencil line stays exactly where it was drawn.

Question 4. The solvent level is above the baseline. What will happen?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

A. The dye spots will dissolve into the solvent in the beaker [1 mark]

B is incorrect. The speed of solvent rising depends on the paper and the solvent, not on where the baseline starts.

C is incorrect. With the dye washed off into the beaker, no Rf values can be calculated, so the result is the opposite of accurate.

D is incorrect. The paper will still get wet when it is dipped in the solvent.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

This is the most common mistake when setting up paper chromatography. The dye spots must always sit above the solvent level, so the rising solvent can pick them up and carry them up the paper.

If the solvent starts above the dye spots, the dyes dissolve straight into the solvent in the beaker and the chromatogram never forms.

Question 5. A dye that has stayed on the baseline. What does this tell you?
Mark Scheme and Guidance

B. The dye did not dissolve in the solvent [1 mark]

A is incorrect. The most soluble dye travels the furthest up the paper, not the shortest.

C is incorrect. A dye on the baseline has an Rf of 0, which is the lowest possible value, not the highest.

D is incorrect. A dye that reaches the solvent front has an Rf close to 1, not 0.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

A dye that does not move means the solvent could not dissolve it. To identify this dye, the experiment would need to be repeated using a different solvent, one that can dissolve this particular dye.

Section B. Structured Question

Question (a)(i). Describe how simple distillation separates the solvent from the dyes. Refer to a change of state. [3 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The procedure separates the solvent from the dyes as follows:

  • The ink is heated in the flask, and the solvent boils / evaporates [1 mark]
  • The solvent vapour enters the condenser, where it cools and condenses back into a liquid [1 mark]
  • The liquid solvent drips into the receiving beaker, and the dyes are left behind in the flask [1 mark]

"Boils" and "evaporates" are both accepted.

"Melts" is not accepted. The change of state is liquid to gas and back to liquid, not solid to liquid.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The command word is describe, so write the story of the solvent in order: liquid in the flask, then vapour in the side-arm, then back to liquid in the condenser, then collected in the beaker.

The phrase "change of state" in the question is a hint that the marker wants the words boils (or evaporates) and condenses. Without these two words, you may drop a mark.

Question (a)(ii). The thermometer holds steady at 100 °C. State, with a reason, what this tells the student about the solvent. [2 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The conclusion and the reason are:

  • The solvent is water [1 mark]
  • Because the boiling point of water is 100 °C, and the steady thermometer reading shows the boiling point of the liquid that is vaporising [1 mark]

Saying "the boiling point is 100 °C" without identifying the solvent does not gain the first mark.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

"State, with a reason" is a two-mark prompt. One mark for the conclusion (water), one mark for the reason (water boils at 100 °C). Always check the question for these compound command words and write a clear two-part answer.

Question (a)(iii). Give one reason why the receiving end is left open. [1 mark]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

One reason the receiver is open to the atmosphere:

  • If sealed, pressure would build up inside the apparatus as the solvent boils, which could crack the glass / blow the bung out [1 mark]

"To let the gas out" alone does not gain the mark. The answer must mention the consequence (pressure build-up or breakage).

Examiner Tips and Tricks

Safety questions need a clear chain of cause and effect. Boiling makes vapour. If the vapour cannot escape, pressure builds up. Pressure cracks glass. Write at least two links of that chain so the marker can see your reasoning.

Question (b)(i). Describe three things the student must do when preparing the chromatography paper, before lowering it into the solvent. [3 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

Any three from (1 mark for each point):

  • Draw a baseline in pencil (about 1 cm from the bottom of the paper)
  • Place a small concentrated spot of the ink on the baseline
  • Add reference spots of known dyes on the same baseline, away from the ink spot
  • Allow the spots to dry before placing the paper in the solvent
  • Avoid touching the surface of the paper where the spots will travel

[3 marks]

"Draw a baseline" without specifying pencil does not gain the mark. Pen ink would dissolve and run up the paper.

Lowering the paper into the solvent is not a preparation step and does not gain credit on this part.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The question asks about preparing the paper, not running the chromatogram. Steps that happen after the paper enters the solvent (solvent rising, marking the front, removing the paper) are outside the scope of this part.

The two easiest marks to bank are "pencil baseline" and "small spot of ink". Both are points examiners often look for.

Question (b)(ii). A dye moves 30 mm. The solvent front moves 60 mm. Calculate the Rf value to 2 significant figures. [2 marks]

Model Answer

To calculate the Rf value of the dye:

  • Write down the Rf formula:

    Rf = distance moved by dye ÷ distance moved by solvent front

  • Substitute the measured distances:

    Rf = 30 ÷ 60

    Rf = 0.50 [1 mark]

  • Quote the final answer to 2 significant figures:

    Rf = 0.50 [1 mark]

Mark Scheme and Guidance

1 mark for the calculation 30 ÷ 60 = 0.50 (or equivalent decimal value).

1 mark for the final answer quoted correctly to 2 significant figures.

An answer of "0.5" is not accepted for the second mark. This is only 1 significant figure; the trailing zero in 0.50 is required.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

This is the kind of calculation that gives easy marks if you stick to two rules. First, divide dye distance by solvent distance. Second, round to 2 significant figures.

Watch the trailing zero. 0.50 and 0.5 are the same number, but in chemistry exams they signal different precision. 2 significant figures means two digits, so 0.50 is required.

Rf is a ratio of two distances and has no units. Do not write "0.50 mm" or "0.50 cm".

Question (b)(iii). One dye has stayed on the baseline. Suggest one change and explain why. [2 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

The change and the reason are:

  • Repeat the chromatography using a different solvent [1 mark]
  • Because the new solvent can dissolve the dye that the original solvent could not, so the dye is carried up the paper and produces a spot on the chromatogram [1 mark]

"Use a different solvent" alone does not gain the second marking point. The reason must mention that the new solvent dissolves the dye that the original could not.

"Heat the solvent" or "wait longer" are not accepted.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

The shape of a top answer is always two halves. First, the change ("use a different solvent"). Second, the reason ("because the new solvent can dissolve the dye that the original could not, so the dye now travels up the paper").

Write both halves clearly and the marker has no choice but to award both marks.

Question (c). Apart from changing the solvent, suggest one improvement and explain why it helps. [2 marks]
Mark Scheme and Guidance

Any one improvement, with its matching reason (1 mark for the improvement, 1 mark for the reason):

  • Improvement: cover the beaker with a lid (or watch-glass) during the run.
    Reason: to stop the solvent evaporating, so the solvent front rises further and the Rf value is reliable.
  • Improvement: mark the solvent front in pencil straight after removing the paper.
    Reason: the solvent front evaporates quickly and becomes invisible, so Rf cannot be measured if it is lost.
  • Improvement: measure to the centre of each spot rather than the edge.
    Reason: this gives a consistent reading and avoids overestimating how far the dye has moved.
  • Improvement: keep the spots small (or re-spot the ink several times).
    Reason: small spots prevent dyes overlapping, so the centre of each spot is easier to measure.

[2 marks]

"Use a better paper" or "use more solvent" are not accepted without a clear reason.

Examiner Tips and Tricks

Pair every improvement with its matching reason. A sentence of the form "do X, so that Y" gives the marker both marks in one go.

The lid-on-tank improvement is the safest choice. It is in the specification and easy to explain in one short sentence.
