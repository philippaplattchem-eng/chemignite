Examiner must-knows by topic
Topic 1: Atomic Structure
  • Isotope = same element (same protons) + different neutrons. Both halves must appear in your answer.
  • Period = horizontal row. Group = vertical column. These are not interchangeable.
  • For an ion such as X²⁻ with 54 electrons: subtract the charge to find protons. 54 − 2 = 52 protons.
  • Relative atomic mass is a weighted mean and will not always be a whole number; do not round it.
Topic 2: Bonding
  • Ionic bonding = electron transfer (not sharing). Draw a dot-and-cross showing one atom gaining and one losing.
  • An ionic lattice contains "alternating positive and negative ions." Use the word "lattice."
  • Ionic compounds conduct when molten or dissolved because ions are free to move.
  • CO₂ has a low boiling point because it has weak intermolecular forces, despite strong covalent bonds.
Topic 3: Quantitative Chemistry
  • To convert cm³ to dm³: divide by 1000. This step is the most frequently penalised on Paper 1.
  • Mr of CaCO₃ = 40 + 12 + (3 × 16) = 100. The subscript 3 multiplies the whole CO₃.
  • Conservation of mass: if mass decreases, a gas was produced and escaped the open flask.
  • Empirical formula: divide each mass by its Ar, then find the simplest whole-number ratio.
Topic 4: Chemical Changes
  • Ammonia = NH₃. Three hydrogen atoms, not two or four.
  • Hydrogen gas: hold a lit splint to the mouth of the tube. A squeaky pop confirms hydrogen. Oxygen: a glowing splint relights.
  • Testing for sulfates: add dilute HCl to remove carbonates, then add BaCl₂ solution. White precipitate = sulfate.
  • Phenolphthalein: colourless in acid, pink/magenta in alkali.
Topic 5: Energy Changes
  • Activation energy arrow starts at the reactant energy level and ends at the peak of the curve.
  • Bond breaking requires energy (endothermic). Bond making releases energy (exothermic).
  • A catalyst lowers the activation energy by providing an alternative reaction pathway.
  • If overall energy released (bonds made) > energy absorbed (bonds broken), the reaction is exothermic.
Quick-check: 5 easy marks to stop losing
  • Write the unit after every calculation answer: cm³, dm³, kJ/mol, g/mol.
  • Re-read the question after writing your answer. Check the command word and mark allocation.
  • Name reactions and processes: "neutralisation," "electrolysis," "precipitation."
  • Show all calculation steps. Method marks are awarded even if the final answer is wrong.
  • Check rounding: if the question says "1 decimal place," give 1 decimal place.
