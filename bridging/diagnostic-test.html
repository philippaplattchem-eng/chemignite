Chemistry

Year 12 Diagnostic Test
A quick check of the GCSE skills you need before starting A Level. Designed to find your gaps, not to grade you.
Time allowed: 30 minutes
Section A: 15 multiple-choice questions [15 marks]
Section B: 5 short structured questions [15 marks]
Total: 30 marks
For students starting
OCR A Level Chemistry A (H032 / H432) Chemistry Department

Instructions

  • You may use a calculator and a Periodic Table.
  • Answer all questions.
  • For multiple-choice questions, put a cross in the box of the correct answer.
  • Where calculations are required, show your working.
  • The marks available for each question are shown in [brackets].
How to use this test: when you finish, mark your own paper using the mark scheme on the back. Then fill in the "Where are my gaps?" grid. Each question maps to a specific GCSE skill, and the grid tells you exactly which section of the transition booklet to revise. The goal is to walk into your first Y12 lesson knowing what you already have nailed, and what you need to brush up on.

Working for selected MCQs

QReasoning
1Neutral atom: electrons = protons = atomic number = 15.
3RAM = (0.507 × 79) + (0.493 × 81) = 40.05 + 39.93 = 79.99 ≈ 80.0.
4n = m / M = 4.0 / 40.0 = 0.10 mol.
5m = n × M = 0.250 × 100.0 = 25.0 g.
6V = n × Vm = 0.100 × 24.0 = 2.40 dm3.
7Convert volume: 100 cm3 = 0.100 dm3. c = n / V = 0.020 / 0.100 = 0.20 mol dm−3.
8Divide each % by Ar: C: 75.0/12.0 = 6.25; H: 25.0/1.0 = 25. Ratio C : H = 1 : 4 → CH4.
9% yield = (actual/theoretical) × 100 = (6.40/8.00) × 100 = 80.0%.
11Bromine, Br2, undergoes addition across the C=C bond in an alkene, removing the orange colour of bromine water. Limewater tests for CO2; damp red litmus tests for NH3; silver nitrate tests for halides. Answer: C.
