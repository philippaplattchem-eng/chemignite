Paper 3 — Unified Chemistry H432/03
70 marks 1 hr 30 min 15 MCQ + structured Qs 26% of A Level
Paper 3 is synoptic — it draws on content from all modules (1–6) and links topics together in a single scenario or question. You may be asked to apply knowledge from multiple modules in one answer. Any topic from Papers 1 or 2 can appear here, often in combination.
Module 1 Development of Practical Skills
Paper 3 has a strong emphasis on practical skills — questions often require you to plan, analyse or evaluate experimental data across multiple topic areas.
Module 2 Foundations in Chemistry
All Module 2 topics (moles, bonding, structure, redox, acids) may appear synoptically — often as the calculation or quantitative part of a longer question.
Module 3 The Periodic Table and Energy
Enthalpy, equilibrium, rates and periodicity topics from Module 3 frequently appear alongside Module 5 content in synoptic questions (e.g. Kc linked to Kp, or Boltzmann distribution linked to Arrhenius).
Module 4 Core Organic Chemistry
Organic mechanisms, synthesis routes and analytical techniques from Module 4 may be tested in combination with Module 6 content — e.g. identifying a compound using MS, IR and NMR together.
Module 5 Physical Chemistry and Transition Elements
Arrhenius, Kp, Born–Haber, ΔG, electrode potentials and transition metal chemistry all appear synoptically — often combined with thermodynamic or equilibrium reasoning from other modules.
Module 6 Organic Chemistry and Analysis
Advanced organic synthesis, spectroscopic analysis (NMR, MS, IR, GC) and chromatography are common synoptic contexts — often linked to biochemistry or materials science scenarios.
