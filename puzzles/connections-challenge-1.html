Connections Challenge 1 — ANSWERS

CHALLENGE

Key traps: CONDENSATION looks like it belongs with separation techniques (condensation is a step in distillation) — it is actually exothermic. CRYSTALLISATION looks endothermic — it is actually a separation technique. EVAPORATION is used in separation but here belongs with endothermic processes.

★★☆☆   GROUP 1 — Exothermic processes
All release energy to the surroundings. Combustion and neutralisation are chemical; condensation and freezing are physical phase changes.
COMBUSTION
NEUTRALISATION
CONDENSATION
FREEZING
Trap: CONDENSATION is also a step in distillation — but the process itself is exothermic (gas → liquid releases energy).
★★★☆   GROUP 2 — Endothermic processes
All absorb energy from the surroundings. Melting and evaporation are physical; photosynthesis and electrolysis are chemical.
MELTING
EVAPORATION
PHOTOSYNTHESIS
ELECTROLYSIS
Trap: EVAPORATION is used in some separation techniques, but the process itself is endothermic (liquid → gas absorbs energy).
★★☆☆   GROUP 3 — Separation techniques
All are methods used to separate mixtures in the laboratory.
FILTRATION
DISTILLATION
CHROMATOGRAPHY
CRYSTALLISATION
Trap: CRYSTALLISATION involves solidification (which releases energy) — but its role here is as a separation/purification technique.
★★★★   GROUP 4 — Types of chemical formula
All describe different ways to represent a compound's formula. Empirical (simplest ratio), molecular (actual number of atoms), structural (shows bonds), displayed (shows all bonds and atoms).
EMPIRICAL
MOLECULAR
STRUCTURAL
DISPLAYED
Trap: these are adjectives used before "formula" — students may not spot the linking word.
